# Milestone 1 — community notes

**Community:** Hacker News comments (news.ycombinator.com). Public, no login
needed to read, thousands of comments a day, and a very wide spread in how much
a comment actually contributes. Comments are spoiler-free and readable
without having followed one particular show or release.

**What I read:** 40 comments sampled at random from 40 busy front-page threads
(150+ comments each) from the past month — model releases, Android/AOSP, a
NixOS government project, Shopify leaving React Native, a coffee machine that
used 1 TB of data, and so on.

## Candidate distinctions I saw in the text

1. **Some comments argue from what the writer did themselves.** "I'm running
   the Q8 on a 256GB system and compared it head to head vs a Q4 on a 128GB
   system, and the Q4 is just plain not as good." The support is the writer's
   own use, not something you could look up.
2. **Some argue from facts or reasoning outside the writer.** "Germany had its
   Bundeswehr the whole time since it joined NATO in 1955 … there are not even
   remotely 200k US troops in Germany anymore." Checkable, and not about the
   writer.
3. **Many state a verdict and stop.** "It seems VC money is not really
   compatible with open source." "It is not Intelligence. It is pattern
   matching." Confident, sometimes right, never supported.
4. **A big chunk isn't about the topic at all.** Thanks, praise for the
   poster's project ("This is pretty cool and it's pure art"), jabs at
   another commenter ("Wow you're really 'irked'…"), and "what did you mean?"
   questions. These are about the conversation, not the subject.

What I did *not* see as a usable distinction: tone. Heated comments were spread
evenly across all four groups above, so "angry vs calm" would cut across the
interesting line rather than along it.
